Marketing Opportunities

Start with a real Windows problem, not a generic sales pitch

Strong ProtectKit content can teach something useful even before the viewer decides to download the application.

“What is connecting to the internet from my PC?”

Demonstrate Live Connections and explain how processes, ports, addresses, signatures, hashes, and destination information can provide context.

“What starts automatically with Windows?”

Show how users can review startup entries, publishers, executable paths, signatures, and other evidence.

“What are all these Windows services?”

Teach users why unfamiliar service names are common and how ProtectKit helps investigate them before making changes.

“How secure are my Windows settings?”

Cover Defender, Firewall, DNS Protection, Windows Hardening, System Change verification, and other supported controls.

“Could unwanted monitoring software be installed?”

Discuss spyware and stalkerware awareness responsibly. Emphasize evidence and investigation rather than claiming someone is being monitored without proof.

“What does suspicious computer behavior look like?”

Explain intrusion monitoring, ransomware-like behavior, suspicious connections, and why multiple signals are stronger than one unusual event.

“Can Windows repair itself?”

Demonstrate ProtectKit's guided access to Windows cleanup, repair, optimization, restore, and recovery workflows without exaggerated performance claims.

“I want Windows security explained in plain English.”

Focus on ProtectKit's central idea: evidence, context, confidence, and practical next actions instead of unexplained technical warnings.

Promote ProtectKit Responsibly

Build trust instead of using scare tactics

ProtectKit affiliates should accurately represent the product. Security marketing can cause unnecessary fear when claims go beyond the available evidence, so ProtectKit does not encourage deceptive warnings or invented threats.

Good affiliate practices

  • Demonstrate real ProtectKit features.
  • Describe your genuine experience with the product.
  • Explain both Free and Premium accurately.
  • Teach users how to investigate before taking destructive action.
  • Use approved ProtectKit logos, screenshots, and product descriptions.
  • Clearly disclose your affiliate relationship.
  • Make claims you can support.
  • Encourage downloads from official ProtectKit sources.

Do not promote ProtectKit this way

  • Do not falsely tell someone their PC is infected.
  • Do not create fake virus or security warnings.
  • Do not claim ProtectKit guarantees complete protection.
  • Do not claim an unfamiliar process is malware without evidence.
  • Do not imply you are ProtectKit or an official ProtectKit employee.
  • Do not hide your financial relationship when disclosure is required.
  • Do not spam people or use deceptive redirects.
  • Do not make promises that ProtectKit itself does not make.
Affiliate disclosure

If you receive commissions from ProtectKit referrals, clearly disclose that financial relationship where required. In the United States, the FTC's endorsement guidance requires material connections between an endorser and a brand to be disclosed when consumers would not otherwise expect the connection.

Example: “I may earn a commission if you purchase ProtectKit Premium through my link.”

When are affiliate commissions paid?

Can I claim ProtectKit found malware on someone's computer?

Do I have to disclose that I am an affiliate?
