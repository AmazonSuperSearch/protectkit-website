Promote ProtectKit Responsibly

Build trust instead of using scare tactics

ProtectKit affiliates should accurately represent the product. Security marketing can cause unnecessary fear when claims go beyond the available evidence, so ProtectKit does not encourage deceptive warnings or invented threats.

Good affiliate practices

  • Demonstrate real ProtectKit features.
  • Describe your genuine experience with the product.
  • Explain both Free and Premium accurately.
  • Teach users how to investigate before taking destructive action.
  • Use approved ProtectKit logos, screenshots, and product descriptions.
  • Clearly disclose your affiliate relationship.
  • Make claims you can support.
  • Encourage downloads from official ProtectKit sources.

Do not promote ProtectKit this way

  • Do not falsely tell someone their PC is infected.
  • Do not create fake virus or security warnings.
  • Do not claim ProtectKit guarantees complete protection.
  • Do not claim an unfamiliar process is malware without evidence.
  • Do not claim ProtectKit proves who is spying on someone or identifies an attacker unless the available evidence actually establishes that conclusion.
  • Do not imply you are ProtectKit or an official ProtectKit employee.
  • Do not hide your financial relationship when disclosure is required.
  • Do not spam people or use deceptive redirects.
  • Do not make promises that ProtectKit itself does not make.
Affiliate disclosure

If you receive commissions from ProtectKit referrals, clearly disclose that financial relationship where required. In the United States, the FTC's endorsement guidance requires material connections between an endorser and a brand to be disclosed when consumers would not otherwise expect the connection.

Example: “I may earn a commission if you purchase ProtectKit Premium through my link.”
